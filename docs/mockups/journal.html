The Shallows

The Net's cacophony of stimuli short‑circuits both conscious and unconscious thought, preventing our minds from thinking either deeply or creatively.

We are evolving from being cultivators of personal knowledge to being hunters and gatherers in the electronic data forest.

Our brains become adept at forgetting, inept at remembering. Our growing dependence on the Net's information stores may in fact be the product of a self‑perpetuating loop.

The two round‑trip buttons sit in the top bar: Reading on the journal, Journal in the book. Same place, both ways.

What the screens decide

The microphone dock, not a floating button

A thought dies in about twenty seconds, so capture is one tap from the journal's front door. Hold to speak a short thought, or tap once for a long ramble and tap again to stop.

Grey words are guesses, black words are kept

The speaking screen shows the tail of the sentence in grey italic while the recogniser is still deciding, then settles it in full black with its punctuation. That is exactly how the browser hands the words over, and showing it is honest about what the app knows.

The screen stays awake, and says so

A phone dims after about thirty seconds of no touching, which is fatal for a two‑minute ramble. The app asks the browser for a wake lock while the microphone is open and shows the small chip so you can see it holding.

Raw is a peer of Tidied, not a hidden detail

The two sit in one switch at the top of the entry. Raw is never rewritten. Tidied can be regenerated or deleted and nothing is lost.

A book‑linked entry keeps its passage

The entry shows the lines it grew from, and a faint warm tint marks it as belonging to a book. The tie is stored as the words of the passage, so a re‑parse cannot strand it.

Spoken notes join the selection popup

Define, Ask Veda and Highlight already live there. Speak is the fourth, and it is the only one that writes a journal entry.
