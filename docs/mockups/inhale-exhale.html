What fills each half, and why

Your note was that the first version had empty space. It did. Everything below earns its place by telling you something you would otherwise have to go and find.

The reading half

  • Chapter, name and percent. Where you stopped, without opening the book.
  • Your last mark, in reading serif. The book speaks on the front door.
  • The minutes ring. 24 of 30 today. One glance, no screen to visit.
  • Cover and Resume. Two targets for one job, because thumbs differ.
  • “3 marks in this chapter” and Library (14). A reason to go wider.

The journal half

  • A written prompt. This is Argon's best small idea. A blank page is a wall. A question is a door.
  • The microphone, named. Speaking is the main way in, so it is large and it says what it does.
  • Yesterday's first line, and 42 entries. The journal remembers you.

What opening a half adds

The accordion is not only stretch. Open the reading half and the shelf appears. Open the journal and today's entries appear. The extra room carries extra content, so the fold has a purpose.

Kept from Argon

The inhale and exhale names, which say why both halves live in one app. The living lip, so you are never shut inside one mode. The bridge on the seam, which carries your last mark into a new entry. Tapping straight through, with no menu between you and the page.

Changed

Argon used Instrument Serif, Inter, JetBrains Mono and amber on slate, loaded through Tailwind from a CDN. This uses your Playfair Display, EB Garamond and system font, your accent that turns from brown to gold at night, and your geometric glyphs in place of emoji. Same ideas, your materials.

Left out, on purpose: the streak

Argon shows a 5‑day streak with a flame. Turn the third switch on to see it. My advice is to leave it off. A streak turns a journal into a duty. On the night you do not feel like writing, a streak tells you that you failed, and that is the night people stop. The entry count and yesterday's line give you the same sense of continuity without the threat.

Still my one disagreement: the clock

Argon leans the split by the hour. I would lean it by what you did last. Your own habit is better evidence than the time of day, and a wrong guess from your behaviour corrects itself the next time you use the app.
