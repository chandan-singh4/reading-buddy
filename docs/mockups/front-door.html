Five front doors

The same decision drawn five ways: you open the app, and you choose reading or journaling. Each one is a different idea, not a restyle of the one before it.

The app leans towards whatever you did last. Flip it:
Changes doors 1 and 3.

The idea
Two cards, one above the other. The one you used last is the taller of the two.
Why it works
Nothing is hidden and nothing is guessed. It also fits the app you already have, so it is the cheapest to build.
The risk
It is the version any app would arrive at. Safe, and a little plain.

02 The open book

The idea
The screen is a book lying open. Left leaf reads, right leaf journals, with the gutter shadow down the middle.
Why it works
The metaphor is the app. You already draw paper grain and a gutter shadow in the reader, so this reuses a texture the app owns rather than inventing one.
The risk
Two narrow halves on a phone. Fine for two doors, no room for a third.

The idea
No cards at all. The cover bleeds across the top, the journal sits on plain paper below. The split shifts to favour whichever you used last.
Why it works
The book itself is the button. Your own notes say you want the shelf to feel illustrated, warm and cover‑forward. This is that, applied to the front door.
The risk
It depends on a good cover. A book with no cover art leaves a dull slab across the top.

The idea
Almost nothing on the screen. A greeting and two words, set large in the display face.
Why it works
It asks one question and gets out of the way. The quietest of the five, and the fastest to read in the dark.
The risk
It shows you nothing: no cover, no progress, no sense of the day. Sparse can tip into bare.

05 The desk

The idea
Two objects set down on a desk: the book you are reading, and a closed notebook with a ribbon. You tap the thing, not a button.
Why it works
It is the only one with any warmth in it. It reads as a place you come back to rather than a menu you operate.
The risk
The most work, and the easiest to get wrong. Drawn badly it looks like a toy, and it needs real cover art to pay off.

If you want my pick

Door 3, two halves. It gives you the one thing the others do not: the book is physically there on the front door, large, with its own cover. That answers “what am I reading?” and “do I want to read?” in the same glance, and your notes already say you want the app to feel cover‑forward.

Door 5 is the one I would most like to build, and the one I would warn you about. It needs illustration work and good covers to be charming instead of twee.

Door 1 is the safe answer and there is no shame in it. It is half a day of work and it will not be wrong.

One thing worth keeping whichever you pick: the small way back to the Library and to the full journal. The front door picks a mood. It should not become the only way in.
